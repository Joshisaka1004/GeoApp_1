## GR 01 – Blue = 73
1. The four blue pieces plus the white triangle below the top middle form one big triangle: apex bottom right, base = top edge of the big square.
2. Sliding a triangle's apex along the ground keeps its area (same base, same height).
3. Big triangle: slide the apex to the left corner → half of the 14-square = 98.
4. White triangle: base 5 (half of the top edge), height 10 → ½ · 5 · 10 = 25.
5. Blue = 98 − 25 = 73. The small square is never needed.

## GR 02 – x = 6°
1. Two angles 3x at the bottom: the lower triangle is isosceles, so both segments to the inner point are equal.
2. With the tick marks, the left side has the same length too. Join the inner point to the top vertex: the new left triangle is isosceles with apex angle 4x.
3. Its altitude halves the angle (2x) and the new segment.
4. Drop a perpendicular from the inner point to the right side. The two yellow right triangles have equal hypotenuses and equal angles 2x → they are congruent.
5. So the distance to the right side is half the joining segment → a 30° angle (half an equilateral triangle).
6. Angle sum in the triangle inner point – top – right corner: 2x + 30° + (90° + 8x) = 180° → x = 6°.

## GR 03 – exactly one half
1. A semicircle on a segment d has area π/8 · d². So does an eighth circle of radius d. Each blue piece is π/8 · (its chord piece)².
2. For two perpendicular chords, the four squared pieces always add up to the squared diameter (Archimedes' lemma: the arcs of the two hidden chords make a semicircle → AB² + CD² = diameter²).
3. Blue = π/8 · 4R² = πR²/2 → exactly half the circle.

## GR 04 – Total area = 60
1. The two segments are two spokes of a pinwheel; draw all eight (each corner to the midpoint of the side after next).
2. A small regular octagon appears in the middle, surrounded by eight triangles like the yellow one.
3. Helper triangle centre–corner–side midpoint: the spoke is √3 times the inradius r, so its distance from the centre is r/√3.
4. The inner octagon is scaled by 1/√3 → exactly 1/3 of the area.
5. The eight triangles make 2/3, each 1/12 → total = 12 · 5 = 60.

## GR 05 – x = 12
1. The touching point T maps one circle onto the other: the segment from the small circle's foot through T ends exactly at the top of the big circle, and vice versa.
2. Thales in the big circle: the two segments meet at T at a right angle.
3. The four endpoints form a trapezoid with vertical sides (the diameters) and perpendicular diagonals 20 and 15.
4. Slide the 15-segment down by the small diameter: a right triangle with legs 20 and 15 and hypotenuse 25 appears.
5. Its height is exactly x: 20 · 15 = 25 · x → x = 12. (Diameters 9 and 16, 12² = 9 · 16.)

## GR 06 – Prove: A = B + C + 3 · D
Condition (corrected 2 Nov 2026): the point lies on the bottom side, red lies wholly inside the blue triangle, and seen from the point, the midpoint of the diagonal to the top left lies to the left of the other diagonal midpoint. Without this arrangement only the signed version holds: big triangle − both green = ± 4 · Red.

1. Red lies inside the blue triangle, so we must show: whole top triangle − both green triangles = 4 · Red.
2. Let the bottom point slide. Every piece has a fixed base and its apex at the point, whose distance changes evenly. Both sides change evenly – if the equation holds at both ends, it holds everywhere.
3. Point at the left corner: one green piece vanishes. Show: big upper-left triangle − bottom triangle = 4 · Red.
4. The diagonal midpoint E halves the red triangle compared with triangle corner–diagonal–F.
5. F is the midpoint of the other diagonal: its distance to the first diagonal is the average of the distances of the two other corners (one counts negative). This gives exactly the factor 4. Same at the right corner.

## GR 07 – x = 18°
1. Angle sum in the quadrilateral: the two upper angles add up to 240°.
2. Hence the triangle apex–upper left–lower left and the triangle apex–right–lower right have the same angle (60° + upper-left angle) between equal sides.
3. They are congruent: a 60° turn about the roof's apex maps one onto the other.
4. This turn maps the lower left corner onto the lower right one → the triangle apex–base is equilateral as well.
5. So 60° at the lower right → x = 60° − 42° = 18°.

## GR 08 – Red : Blue = 1 : 1
1. Line from the top to the midpoint of the base: both lower corners are equally far from it → Blue = helper triangle next to it.
2. Hidden parallels (midlines) show: the line from the top is cut 2 : 2 : 1, and the inner triangle sits on the middle piece.
3. Likewise the right inner point halves the segment from the right corner.
4. Red has double the base of the helper triangle but half the height → equal areas.
5. Red = Blue = 1/10 of the triangle, for every triangle shape.

## GR 09 – Green = 25
1. Draw the missing line between the two roof tips: two small trapezoids appear.
2. In a trapezoid with diagonals, the two side triangles are equal, and their square is the product of the top and bottom triangles.
3. Left trapezoid: 18 · 8 = 144 = 12² → the left half of the 27 is 12.
4. So the right half is 27 − 12 = 15.
5. Right trapezoid: Green · 9 = 15² = 225 → Green = 25.

## GR 10 – Green = 33
1. The circle passes through the lower left corner, where there is a right angle → the two crossing points form a diameter (Thales); a rectangle with that corner sits inside the circle.
2. The centre is at half the height of the left piece, and so is the touching point on the right. With side s: bottom piece s − 1, left piece s − 3.
3. Tangent from the lower right corner: ((s − 3)/2)² = 1 · s → s = 9. Rectangle 8 × 6, diameter 10.
4. A 180° turn about the centre moves the green cap below onto the white cap at the top, and the green cap on the left onto the white cap on the right.
5. Afterwards everything is green except the rectangle: Green = 81 − 48 = 33.

## GR 11 – x = 58°
1. Extend the line from the lower left corner through the side midpoint until it meets the extended top side.
2. The two triangles at the midpoint are congruent → the midpoint also halves the extended line.
3. The line with the right angle is perpendicular at that midpoint → the yellow triangle is isosceles.
4. So the line halves the lower left angle. The whole angle is 64° (alternate angle to the top) → half is 32°.
5. x = 90° − 32° = 58°.

## GR 12 – x = 13
1. Call the outer squares' sides a (left) and b (right).
2. Under the tilted square sit two equal right triangles with legs a and b → x² = a² + b².
3. The same triangle appears again on top. Left gable: 2a across, b up → 22² = 4a² + b².
4. Right gable: 2b across, a up → 19² = a² + 4b².
5. Add: 845 = 5 · (a² + b²) = 5 · x² → x = 13. The outer squares are never needed.

## GR 13 – 5/6 shaded
1. The four equal pieces at the bottom show: the roof rises 2 for every 1 across.
2. Next to each tower square, the two corner triangles together are half the square → corners = 1/3 of every level.
3. Above every tower square sits a half-size copy of the whole → corners = 1/3 of the whole triangle.
4. Each white tooth has sides d and 2d, so its area is d² – exactly the square below it.
5. White = half the corners = 1/6, shaded = 5/6.

## GR 14 – x = 25°
1. The tent tip is equally far from both lower corners and sees the base under 4x = 2 · 2x.
2. Only the centre of the circle through all three corners does that → the missing line to the right corner is as long as the tent poles.
3. The right side has the same tick → the yellow triangle is equilateral (60° at the tip).
4. On the circle the angle is half as big → 30° at the lower left.
5. Exterior angle: 6x = 180° − 30° = 150° → x = 25°.

## GR 15 – Green = 28
1. Mark the centre of the green square and join it to the four big corners.
2. Each frame piece = triangle to the big side − a quarter of green ± two thin slivers.
3. Opposite triangles to the big sides make half the big square (their heights add up to 10).
4. The blue and the orange slivers are equal (a quarter turn maps one pair onto the other).
5. So two opposite frame pieces = half the frame: 13 + 23 = 36 = (100 − Green) : 2 → Green = 28.

## GR 16 – Red : Blue = 1 : 2
1. The height splits the right triangle into two scaled copies; each small square is a scaled copy of the big one.
2. Pythagoras for the scaling: the two small squares together equal the big one.
3. Blue = small squares minus their parts inside the big one = big square minus the same parts = Red + the free white quadrilateral at the top right of the big square (yellow in the solution picture).
4. The tip of Red is a corner of both small squares, with a right angle there; the three squares are the Pythagoras squares of the red triangle. Extended, the sides of Red meet the right side and the top side of the big square and bound the white quadrilateral.
5. Turn Red by 90° about the centre of the big square. It covers the white quadrilateral except for a triangle at the top right, and instead sticks into the right small square with a triangle at the bottom right (exactly that square's part inside the big one). Turning this piece another 90° fills the gap exactly. So White = Red and Blue = 2 · Red.
